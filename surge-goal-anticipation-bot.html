The xG problem

API-Football does not return xG Its statistics endpoint gives shots on goal, off goal, total, blocked, inside box, outside box, corners, possession, saves, passes. No expected goals on any plan.
pxG = 0.150 × shotsInsideBox + 0.040 × shotsOutsideBox
Calibration target2.65 per match
Against league goals2.70 per match

Statistics arrive cumulative, so keep a rolling buffer and subtract to get a ten-minute window. Call /leagues?id= first — a competition with no statistics coverage returns empty arrays and your tempo sits flat at ×1.00 forever.

Possessioni

Raw possession barely predicts goals — a side can hold 70% passing sideways, and a team protecting a lead gives the ball away on purpose. What carries information is whether the side on the ball is the side that needs a goal. The scoreline alone cannot see that.

dom = (possHome − 50) / 50 chase = −dom × sign(homeGoals − awayGoals) mult = 1 + weight × chase (0.82–1.22)
Possession influence0.18
Zero ignores possession entirely. Deliberately modest — it is the most over-read number in football.

Sterile domination needs no term of its own: lots of the ball with few shots already shows up as low tempo above.

Attack momentum

Not how high pressure is — whether it is rising. Slope of the pressure index over the last five minutes of match time.

slope = (press_now − press_5min) / 5
Minimum slope+1.5 /min
Price lag → how far to trust the model

The market is usually right. So the model's estimate is blended toward it, and price lag decides the weight: if our probability rose faster than the price, we plausibly know something unpriced. If the price already ran past us, most of the apparent edge is staleness.

lag = (p_now − p_5min) − (mkt_now − mkt_5min) w = clamp(trustBase + trustLag · lag, 0.15, 0.70) pUsed = w · p_model + (1 − w) · p_market
Base trust in the modeli35%
At rest, with no lag either way. Low on purpose — the book has more information than this model does.

Accumulators

Pre-match legs from form. Priced honestly.

What an accumulator actually does It does not create an edge. It multiplies whatever edge you already have — in whichever direction that points. At a 5% margin per leg you are giving up 21.6% on a four-fold and 34% on a six-fold, because the margin compounds too. If your per-leg edge beats the margin, accas amplify the profit. If it doesn't, they amplify the loss far faster than singles would. The slip below works out which of those you're looking at.
